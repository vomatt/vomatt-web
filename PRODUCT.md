# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Members of the public who give their time to ask questions and to answer them. Creators write a poll because they want to know what a community really thinks (residents before a council consultation, a team weighing a policy, a shop association picking a market night). Voters arrive from a feed or a shared link, often on a phone, read the question, pick one option and leave; some stay to explain their choice in the discussion. Both come back when a poll closes to see the result.

## Product Purpose

vomatt is a public voting platform where anyone can create a poll and vote on other people's polls, so the direction of a topic comes from everyone who took part, not from the loudest voice. Ballots stay sealed while a poll is open: people see turnout but not the counts, so nobody votes with the crowd. Success means people trust the result, finish the vote in seconds, and return to see the outcome and ask the next question.

## Brand Personality

Fair, calm, forward-looking. The voice is plain and respectful: short sentences, concrete verbs, no hype. It thanks people for showing up and tells them exactly what happens to their vote. Emotionally it should feel trustworthy and quietly modern, closer to a well-made tool than a social feed.

## Anti-references

- Social-media polls built for engagement: streaks, confetti, live-updating counts that invite herd voting.
- Loud civic or election styling: flag red and blue, ballot-box clip art.
- AI-default looks: purple gradients, glowing buttons, glass cards, serif-on-cream editorial.
- Survey tools with dense, form-heavy pages and grey enterprise chrome.

## Design Principles

1. **The question is the hero.** Every screen leads with the question and the next action; chrome stays quiet.
2. **Trust is shown, not claimed.** Explain sealing, visibility and one-vote rules at the moment they matter, in one plain sentence.
3. **Every voice is acknowledged.** Voting and creating always end in a clear confirmation and a thank-you, and results go back to the people who voted.
4. **Equal weight for everyone.** No visual hierarchy of users by follower count or status; turnout is celebrated, popularity is not.
5. **Familiar over clever.** Standard controls and patterns so first-time voters never hesitate.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.2 AA: 4.5:1 text contrast, 3:1 for UI components and focus indicators, visible keyboard focus, 44px minimum touch targets, real form labels, radio semantics for ballots. Status is never conveyed by color alone (every status has a text label). Respect `prefers-reduced-motion`. Content ships in English and Traditional Chinese, so layouts must tolerate longer and CJK strings.
